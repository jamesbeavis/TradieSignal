# Sales kit: scripts, templates and objection handling

Everything here is written to be copied, edited and sent. Square brackets are
the bits you fill in from the report.

A note on tone before you use any of it: this audience has excellent detectors
for salesmanship and very little patience for it. Every template below is
short, specific, and leads with something useful rather than something
promotional. Keep it that way when you edit them.

---

## 1. The one-project cold email

The highest-converting outreach available, because it is not really outreach —
it is giving someone a lead.

**Method.** Pick a target. Find their base suburb. Open `data/projects.csv`,
filter to projects within about 5 km of them, sort by score, and pick one they
plausibly do not know about — ideally a construction certificate with a named
builder.

```
Subject: 280-unit job on King St, Newcastle West — CC lodged last week

[Name],

You're based in [suburb] so this is probably in your patch.

GWH Build lodged a construction certificate for 309 King Street,
Newcastle West on 9 September. $80.3m, 280 dwellings, 16 storeys.
Electrical on a job that size usually sits somewhere between $7m and
$12m across the whole build.

Council reference is CFT-1024947 if you want to check it yourself.

I pull this out of the NSW planning data every week for Newcastle,
Lake Mac, Maitland, Port Stephens and Cessnock — development
applications, complying development certificates and construction
certificates — and put the ones worth ringing about into a report.

Happy to send you this week's if it's useful. No charge, no sign-up.

[you]
[mobile]
```

**Why it works.** It is verifiable, local, specific about money, and it gives
before it asks. The reference number is doing real work — it says *check me*,
which is the opposite of what a sales email usually says.

**What to avoid.** Do not send the whole report first. One project is
intriguing; a 24-page PDF from a stranger is homework.

## 2. Follow-up after they take the free report

Send two or three days after the report, not the same day.

```
Subject: Re: [original subject]

[Name],

Did anything in there turn out to be useful?

I'm asking properly, not as a sales thing — I'm still working out
which projects are worth putting at the top, and the only way I find
out is by asking people who actually do the work.

If it was useful, it's $49/month for the first twenty people who sign
up, and that price is locked for a year. Month to month, cancel by
replying to any email.

If it wasn't, tell me what was missing and I'll have a go at fixing
it.

[you]
```

The offer is one sentence in the middle. The email is mostly a question,
because at this stage learning is worth more than the $49.

## 3. Facebook group post

Check the group rules first — several ban promotion outright, and getting
removed from *Sparkies Australia* is not recoverable.

The post that works is data, not an ad:

```
Pulled the construction certificates for Newcastle, Lake Mac,
Maitland, Port Stephens and Cessnock for the last fortnight.

180 CCs issued. $76m of declared work. 126 of them name the builder
on the certificate, which most people don't realise is public.

Busiest suburbs by value were Morisset Park, Newcastle West and
Bellbird Heights.

Most active builders: GWH Build (4 jobs), McDonald Jones (28, mostly
project homes), SNL Building Constructions (4).

Happy to post the top 10 by value if anyone wants them.
```

Then post the top 10 when asked. Let people come to you in DMs. Do not put a
link in the original post.

## 4. Wholesaler trade counter

Middendorp, Lawrence & Hanson, MM Electrical, CNW — all have Newcastle counters,
and the counter staff know every contractor in town.

Print 30 copies of the report. Ask the branch manager:

```
"I put together a fortnightly report on what's actually being
approved and certified across the Hunter — council DAs, construction
certificates, who the builders are. It's for sparkies chasing
commercial work.

Can I leave a stack on the counter? Your customers get something
useful, and I'm not selling anything at your counter — my number's on
the back if they want it.

Happy to put your logo on it if you'd rather it came from you."
```

That last line usually gets a yes. Co-branding costs you nothing and turns a
favour into a partnership.

## 5. Industry body approach

NECA NSW Hunter branch, Master Electricians Australia.

Do not sponsor anything yet. Offer content:

```
Subject: 15 minutes of Hunter construction data for a branch meeting

[Name],

I track every development application, complying development
certificate and construction certificate lodged across the five
Hunter councils, out of the NSW Planning Portal's open data.

Last quarter that was [N] approvals worth $[X]m, and I can break it
down by sector, by council and by which builders are most active.

Would a 15-minute slot at a branch meeting be useful to members? No
pitch — just what's in the pipeline and where the work is heading.

[you]
```

You are not selling. You are becoming the person who has the numbers, and in a
small industry that reputation converts on its own.

## 6. Objection handling

### "Can't I just look this up myself?"

Agree immediately. It is the only credible answer.

> Completely, and you should know that. It's public — five council DA trackers
> plus the NSW Planning Portal. Takes about two hours a week once you know
> where to look and what you're looking for. I do it once for everyone and
> charge $49. If you'd rather do it yourself, the councils' trackers are the
> place to start and I mean that genuinely.

Never claim exclusivity. Newcastle is small, someone will check, and the moment
they catch you overstating it you have lost the only asset you have.

### "I'm already flat out."

> Then don't subscribe this month — seriously. Come back when it thins out, and
> it always does. The people who get the most out of this aren't chasing more
> work, they're trying to change the mix — moving from domestic call-outs into
> commercial fitout, or getting onto a builder's regular list instead of
> quoting one-offs.

Turning someone away is the strongest possible signal that you are not
desperate, and they remember it.

### "How do I know the numbers are right?"

> The addresses, dates, approval stages and builder names come straight from
> the government feed — that part is as accurate as what the applicant lodged
> with council. The dollar figures are lodged by applicants and aren't audited,
> so I cross-check every one against floor area and dwelling count. Anything
> that fails is flagged in the report and left out of the totals. This
> fortnight that caught a two-dwelling job in Nelson Bay lodged at $1.375
> billion, which is obviously someone's typo.
>
> The scores and electrical estimates are mine, and I label them as estimates
> every single time. They're for deciding where to spend your week, not for
> pricing a job.

### "$99 is a lot."

> It's about an hour of your charge-out rate. If one commercial fitout comes
> out of it in a year, it's paid for a decade. If nothing does in the first two
> months, cancel — reply to any email and it's done, no phone call.
>
> If it's genuinely tight, start on Starter at $49 for one council. Most people
> move up when they see the builder list.

Never discount below list. Offer a smaller product instead. Discounting teaches
people the price is fiction and it is very hard to walk back.

### "What if my competitors get it too?"

> Some of them will. I'm not going to tell you otherwise. Two things though:
> most electricians never look at this data at all, and the ones who do are
> looking a month too late. And I cap Starter and Professional subscriptions
> per council area so the same list isn't being worked by fifty people.
>
> The edge isn't the data — it's that you ring on Tuesday and they ring in
> October.

### "Send me some information."

Usually a soft no. Answer it with a question instead of a brochure:

> Happy to. What kind of work are you chasing at the moment — commercial
> fitout, new residential, industrial? I'll pull the three best current
> projects in that category near you and send those instead of a brochure.

## 7. Founding subscriber offer

Use for the first twenty. Then stop, and mean it.

```
Founding subscriber — first 20 only

  $49/month, locked for 12 months
  Everything in Professional: all five councils, builder watchlist,
  the lot
  Month to month, cancel by replying to any email
  Your name on the wall, and a say in what gets built next

Why it's cheap: it's early. Right now I compile the report by hand
every Sunday night. The dashboard isn't built. The price reflects
that, and it never goes up for you — including when it goes to $99
for everyone else.

What I want back: tell me what's useful and what isn't. That's the
actual deal.
```

The honesty here is the pitch. "It's early and here's exactly what's missing"
converts better with this audience than any amount of polish, and it sets
expectations you can actually meet.

## 8. Cancellation

One question. Not a survey, not a retention flow, not a discount.

```
Subject: Re: cancelling

No worries at all — it's cancelled, nothing more to do.

One question if you don't mind: what would have made it worth
keeping?

I'm not going to try to talk you back into it. I just want to know.

[you]
```

Log the verbatim answer. Twenty of these is a better product roadmap than any
amount of planning, and a graceful cancellation is a person who might come
back, or refer someone, or say something good about you at a trade counter.
